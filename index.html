ParticleCode prototype v0

An open, color-particle screen-to-camera data channel — inspired by Apple's Quick Start particle cloud, built on plain web tech. Data is encoded in the colors of a swirling particle constellation; any phone camera can decode it.

Live: type here and the cloud re-encodes as you type. Hold a second device on the Receive tab about 20–40 cm from this screen. 160 particles × 3 bits = 60 bytes per state, cycled with Reed–Solomon protection.
Needs a rear camera — requires HTTPS or localhost. Serve this folder (python3 -m http.server) and open on the phone. Bright screen, steady hands, avoid glare. The decoder locks onto the 4 white anchors first, then samples the grid.

The channel

Payload → UTF-8 → 43-byte blocks → [id][total][session][len][data][CRC-16] (49 bytes) → Reed–Solomon(60,49) over GF(256) → 480 bits → 160 three-bit symbols → the color of 160 particles. Blocks cycle continuously; the receiver latches any block that decodes cleanly, in any order, at any time. The session byte bumps on every edit, so live typing on the sender cleanly replaces what the receiver holds.

The codec is payload-agnostic (it carries UTF-8, like QR carries bytes), so it speaks the standard QR payload conventions: URL, WIFI:, vCard, mailto:, tel:, SMSTO:, geo: and otpauth://. The sender builds the standard string from a form; the receiver recognizes it and offers the web-native action — open the link, copy the Wi-Fi password, save a .vcf, and so on.

Why 8 colors and not 24-bit RGB

A camera channel mangles colors: auto white balance, exposure, gamma, chroma-subsampled video. Two decades of color-barcode research (Microsoft's HCCB, COBRA, PixNet…) converged on the same answer — a small, maximally separated palette plus heavy error correction. Six hues on a 60°-spaced wheel (0 60 120 180 240 300) plus two grays give 3 bits/particle that survive the trip.

Structure

  • Anchors — four large white dots in the corners. A close pair top-left disambiguates orientation; the quad drives a homography that rectifies perspective.
  • Calibration satellites — three known hues (R/G/B) at the top edge. Their measured drift estimates the frame's global hue rotation from auto white balance, before classification.
  • Slots — 160 particles sit on a seeded Poisson-disc point cloud (center-dense ellipse, no lattice), so identity comes from geometry — no bits spent on ordering. Sampling discs, wander budget and dot size all adapt to each slot's local spacing; the deterministic point set is shared by encoder and decoder by construction.
  • Timing — states dwell ~1.2 s: 45% swirl, then particles settle exactly onto their anchors. Temporal voting across frames + CRC means blurred transition frames are simply discarded.
  • Dressing — the drifting blue-white motes, faint nebula and motion trails are pure atmosphere (our nod to Apple's Quick Start cloud). The decoder never samples them: they're too small for the anchor filter and lose every symbol vote by ~25:1.
  • Themes — seven scenes (nebula, orbit rings, jet stream, campfire, solar system, clouds, rainstorm) plus Auto, which reads the message's palette and picks its weather. Scenes style the data particles by slot zone — flames, rain streaks, wind darts, cloud puffs — always inside the decode contract; decor is stateless f(t), so the simulator proves each skin decodes.

Honest numbers

~60 bytes/state × ~2–3 states/s ≈ 120–180 B/s after overhead. An animated QR (txqr-style) moves ~50× more data — this trades bandwidth for a format humans enjoy pointing cameras at.
